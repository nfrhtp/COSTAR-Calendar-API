Field Reference & Calendar Concepts

Everything a builder needs to interpret COSTAR Calendar API responses. Endpoints are documented on the main page; the machine-readable spec is at /v1/openapi.json.

The calendar in brief

The COSTAR calendar is a mystical astronomical calendar. It does not replace the Gregorian calendar — it overlays it, assigning every Gregorian day (reckoned in Pacific Time, the calendar's native zone) a position inside nested cycles:

CycleLengthValues
Rotundum25 spiral years Numbered: R0 began at the December 2000 southern solstice, R1 in December 2025, R2 in December 2050. (Days before R0 carry R-1.)
Spiral year (SY)≈1 Gregorian year SY1–SY25 within a rotundum; the year turns on the Southern Solstice day, not January 1. Each SY carries a SY luma.
Quinquennium5 spiral years Alpha (SY1–5), Beta (6–10), Gamma (11–15), Delta (16–20), Epsilon (21–25) — the Q luma.
Lunationnew moon → new moon 12 named lunations per spiral year — White/Black (contains the Southern Solstice), Alpha, Green, Beta, Yellow, Orange, Gamma, Red, Delta, Blue, Epsilon, Purple — plus an occasional 13th, Shen, inserted when needed to keep the solstices and equinoxes in their designated lunations.
Spiral week8 days, Sunday → Sunday Each week carries a week luma: the new-moon week is Epsilon/Alpha, then Beta, Gamma, and Delta go to the weeks containing First Quarter, Full Moon, and Last Quarter. A long lunation's leftover week is a Shen week.
Day1 day A fixed day chroma by weekday, and on five nights a night luma (see tables below).

Transitions vs. resolved values

The calendar's signature idea is the octave: boundaries belong to both sides. A Sunday is day 8 of one spiral week and day 1 of the next; the White/Black lunation ends one spiral year and begins the next. The API gives you both readings of every day:

  • transitions.* — the traditional slash notation, exactly as the calendar writes it: "24/25", "Delta/Epsilon/Alpha", "Purple/White/Black". Strings, verbatim, slashes preserved.
  • costar.* — resolved single values (internally "CD" values), where every overlap is decided by a pivot rule: rotundum/SY pivot on the Southern Solstice day, lunations pivot on the new-moon day (White/Black splits into White before the solstice, Black from it), weeks pivot on Sunday, and the Epsilon/Alpha week splits into Epsilon before the new moon and Alpha from it.

The canonical costarDate string is built from resolved values: R{rotundum} SY{spiralYear} {lunation} {weekLuma} {dayChroma} — e.g. R1 SY1 Red Beta Yellow. Reverse lookup (/v1/costar/…) matches these resolved values only.

Event index objects

  • /v1/events/phases/{year} — date, name, number, timePT, utc, costarDate, lunationNumber per phase event.
  • /v1/events/solar-terms/{year} — date, term, event, timePT, utc, costarDate per term (12 per year).
  • /v1/events/lunations/{year} — date, lunation, timePT, lunationNumber, brownLunation, costarDate per lunation start (new moon).
  • /v1/events/shen — every Shen occurrence in the dataset: lunations (13th-lunation years) and weeks (startSunday of each Shen week).

Practical notes. All dates are Pacific Time days — /v1/today turns over at midnight Pacific, and a phase that falls on, say, July 21 PT may be July 22 in UTC (the utc blocks give the same instants in UTC). Coverage is 2000-11-18 through 2051-01-12; ranges that stick out are clipped and flagged "clipped": true. Data changes only by adjudication — rulings on the calendar's rules — and every change bumps datasetVersion; see the changelog.
